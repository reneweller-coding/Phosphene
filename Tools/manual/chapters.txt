# The prose of the Phosphene manual, one block per tab of the plugin.
#
# Everything else in the manual -- the parameter tables, the pictures, the macro table, the list of
# groups on each page -- is generated from the plugin itself (PHOS_MANUAL writes manual.json and one
# picture per tab). Only the text that explains *why* lives here, because no table can say it.
#
# Format: a line "== Tab name ==" starts a block, and the tab name must be the name the tab wears in
# the plugin. Blank lines separate paragraphs. A line indented by four spaces is printed as it
# stands (a formula, a small table). Lines starting with '#' are comments and are not printed.

== About ==

Phosphene composes and plays complete psytrance sets. You give it a seed, a style and an energy arc;
it writes the tracks -- their keys, tempi, forms, patterns and sounds -- and synthesises them in real
time. There is no sample library and no loop pool: every sound is made by the engine, and every note
is decided by the composer.

Two ideas run through the whole instrument. The first is that the same seed always gives the same
set: bar 3000 is bar 3000 whether the night was played from the start or joined in the middle, which
is what makes a set something you can save in seven lines of text and hand to someone else. The
second is that the composer never writes a knob. It writes offsets from the knobs, so with every
offset at zero the instrument sounds exactly as the panel says, and a variation keeps its musical
size whatever you have set the ranges to.

The panel has a tab per generator, plus four that are about the set rather than the sound: Set,
Arrange, Mixer / Master and Perform. Every control on every page is generated from the engine's own
parameter tables, and so is this manual.

== Set ==

The Set tab is where a night begins. The seed names the set; Randomize seed draws another one. Play
and Stop run Phosphene's own clock, and Follow host hands the transport to the DAW instead -- under a
host the tempo knob follows the host's tempo and the play head follows its position.

Planning a track measures it by rendering parts of it -- its loudness through the master, the
brightness of its lines, how audible each line is against the lead. For the first track of a set that
happens behind the music: it starts playing at once, its intro runs, and the measured levels ride in
over a phrase long before the kick and the lines arrive. A jump to a track that has not been planned
yet still waits for its measurements; the header says "planning" rather than going quiet without
explanation. In the standalone this has usually happened before you press
anything, because the composer thread starts working when the window opens.

The composer's own knobs are here, grouped by what they decide. Start & Style is where the night
begins: the style profile, whether the style walks through the set (Style Mix) and whether each track
takes its style's own tempo (Style Tempo, which then replaces Tempo and Tempo Range), and the start
key and scale. They are called "start" because they are exactly that -- the first track plays them,
and from the second on the key, the tempo and (with Style Mix) the style move from track to track as
a DJ would move them. Set Journey is the shape of the night: the energy arc, the set length, the track
length and how much tracks and sounds vary. Kick & Bass, Rhythm, Melody, Lead and Voices say how much
of each part there is and how it is drawn; Levels holds the three automatic balances -- the loudness
of whole tracks, the presence of the melodic lines, and the audibility of the quiet lines against the
lead.

Export MIDI writes the score of the first N bars as a Standard MIDI File with a named track per part,
section markers and the tempo map. Export set writes a .phosset -- the seed, the style, the arc, the
knobs that differ from their defaults, and every lock and reroll -- which phos_render will play back
byte for byte.

== Arrange ==

The Arrange tab is the whole planned set on one page, and the curation loop that goes with it.

The strip across the top is the night: every planned track a block in the order it plays, the energy
arc drawn over it, the play head in it. Underneath, each track gets a row of its own in which its own
form fills the full width, so a section stays readable no matter how long the set is. The colours are
the section categories: intro and outro grey, groove green, buildup amber, the pre-drop break violet,
the drop red, the breakdown blue. Clicking any block jumps there.

The two small glyphs on every track and every section are the answer to the oldest complaint about
generators, which is that they are boring. The padlock freezes that unit on the seed it has; the die
draws it again. Because a locked unit keeps its old seed and nothing later reads anything an earlier
one decided, rerolling one track leaves every other track bit-identical -- so you can keep what you
like and reroll what you do not, one section at a time, until the set is yours. Reroll the set draws
the whole journey again and leaves the locked tracks exactly where they were.

== Kick ==

The kick is the floor of the genre and the first thing to get right. Two engines: a swept sine whose
pitch falls from Pitch Start to Pitch End with its own decay, and a damped phasor resonator with the
retrigger behaviour of a TR-808. Punch is a second, much faster pitch envelope on top of the first;
Click adds the attack transient; Drive and Clip decide how much of the body survives.

Two of its numbers are not free. The tail is limited so that the kick has fallen far enough by the
time the first bass note starts, and the phase lock (on the Bass tab) can trim the kick's decay so
that the kick and the bass fundamental meet in phase. Both are applied in the engine, after every
knob and every variation.

The Kick Scope renders one hit with the values that play right now (the knobs plus the track's recipe;
stopped, the knobs): its waveform, its pitch sweep on a log axis, its spectrum, and in figures where the
sweep starts, the note it lands on (with its cents off it), its length to -60 dB and its peak.

== Bass ==

The rolling bass and the kick are one instrument with two halves. The oscillator is a PolyBLEP saw
and pulse with a sine sub on the fundamental -- on the fundamental, not an octave down, because the
octave-down sub fought the kick -- and the filter is a non-linear zero-delay ladder.

Kick Lock decides which of the two gives way: Bass follows kick sets the bass start phase to the
kick's phase at that instant, Kick follows bass trims the kick's decay by bisection until the two
agree. Off leaves them where they fall, which at 145 BPM means anything between -157 and +165
degrees.

Pattern and Gate are the note side: which of the five bass patterns the track prefers, and how long a
note is as a fraction of its slot. The gate is never allowed to run shorter than half a period of the
fundamental.

The bass is a pedal on the root. Its figures at the phrase ends may touch the b2 or the leading tone only as a
pickup -- at most two sixteenths, at the end of the bar, into the next downbeat; anywhere else the figure takes
the fifth, because under the drone's tonic those notes are a minor ninth in the low register. Bass Follows
Chords (Set tab) moves the bass to the root the pad holds, and only where the pad plays; the drone moves with
it.

The Bass Scope renders one eighth note of the bass on the key's root with the values that play right now
(the knobs plus the track's recipe; stopped, the knobs): its waveform, the filter's cutoff over the note
as the synth computes it, its spectrum, and the note and the filter's travel in figures.

== Percussion ==

Twelve lanes, each a complete voice: a tone with its own pitch envelope and FM, four modal sets, the
808 square stack, noise with clap bursts, a filter, a low cut, drive, choke and panning. The role of
a lane decides what it plays in the groove and which General MIDI note it exports as.

The patterns are euclidean, rotated to a middle amount of syncopation rather than to none, and the
decisions are made per four-bar phrase. Every lane is high-passed at 150 Hz at the least: the bottom
of the mix belongs to the kick and the bass, and a tom that reaches into it takes the floor away.

== Acid ==

A diode ladder after Zavalishin, solved from the inside out, with an accent that charges a sweep
capacitor the way the original did -- three accents in a row open further than one. Slide is an
exponential glide into the next note, Squelch a cutoff start value plus a comb tuned to the note's
period. The tempo delay is in beats, so it follows the set.

In the Phrygian modes the b2 is a degree of the acid's own: the F of an E - F - E riff, on any step and held
too. The other colour tones -- the upper note of an augmented second -- stay short neighbours that resolve to
the tonic.

== Lead ==

A supersaw after Szabo's measurements of the JP-8000 -- the detune curve, the mix law and the random
phase per note -- with a dynamic detune that follows the length of the note. The other three
oscillator types are a virtual-analogue saw and pulse, two-operator FM and a wavetable.

Everything but the kick and the bass is high-passed at its own fundamental, which is what keeps eight
voices of supersaw out of the bass register.

As in the acid, the Phrygian b2 is one of the lead's degrees, on beats 2 and 4 and off the beat; beats 1 and 3
take a tone of the tonic chord.

== Counter ==

The counter-lead answers the lead. It plays in the gaps the lead leaves and in a register of its own,
so the two never mask each other; Counter Mode on the Set tab (or the style, on Auto) decides whether
it echoes, answers or holds against the lead. The engine is the same as the lead's, with its own
knobs and its own presets.

== Arp ==

The same engine as the lead, with its own knobs. The arpeggio runs on chord tones, and when its pitch
range would overlap the lead's by more than two semitones it is octaved away: two voices playing in
the same two octaves mask each other, and the composer checks that before it writes the notes.

The counter never strikes together with the lead a semitone or a minor ninth apart on beats 1 and 3.

== Pad ==

Four-voice voicings with the smallest voice movement between chords, through the wavetable engine.
The pads carry the blocks that have no lead and no acid, and the trance gate gives them their
movement.

Every pitched voice plays its FM ratio to the nearest half (1, 1.5, 2, ...), so an FM pad or lead is
always harmonic: a ratio in between makes the bell and struck-metal spectra that sounded out of tune
under a chord. A second oscillator stays within a few cents of its interval, and the pad's partner
plays in unison or an octave up, never a fifth, which would stack a second chord on the first.

A pad never holds a minor ninth -- no note a semitone above the tonic that bass and drone play, none a
semitone above its own root -- nor the leading tone under the tonic, nor a tritone over its root. Held for
bars, those intervals sound wrong rather than dark; the genre makes its dissonance with the sound (clusters,
noise, drones), and the Phrygian b2 belongs to the lead and the acid. Where the progression swings to the
bII, the pad stays on the tonic and adds the minor sixth instead (E - B - E - C over E). The major seventh is a
rare colour, on bVI and bIII only.

== Stab ==

Short chord hits on the off-beats and the phrase ends, the punctuation of a groove. They are voiced
from the track's harmony and kept out of the lead's register.

== Drone ==

A long, slowly moving tone under the breakdowns and the intros: the sound that holds a track together
when the kick is gone. It is high-passed above the bass so it never takes the floor. It holds the tonic,
its fifth and its octave, and like the pad never a minor ninth over the bass -- in a DJ blend a note of the
incoming track that would stand a semitone over the outgoing tonic waits for the hand-over. With Bass Follows
Chords on it follows the bass to the pad's root.

== SFX / FX ==

The effect generator makes risers, downlifters, impacts, sweeps, formant shots, reverse swells and
zaps; the composer places them at the form's boundaries -- a riser over the last eight bars of a
buildup, ending exactly on the drop, the formant shot on the last beat of the pre-drop break, a
descending sweep into the drop.

Short one-shots -- zaps, squelches, bubbles, alien chatter -- fall at the ends of phrases, about one
in eight bars of a groove and one in four of the climax; a groove is never left eight bars without an
effect, and what fills such a gap is a sweep or a swell, never a zap.

Effect Presets: every family of the effect bank (2048 presets in eleven families: riser, downlifter,
impact, sweep, formant shot, reverse swell, zap, squelch, bubble, reverse crash, atmosphere) has a
chooser. Auto lets the generator draw a preset of the family for every event, never the same twice in
a track; a number makes every event of that family play that preset. "Hear" plays the chosen preset
in the running set (with Auto, one of the family at random); while the transport stands nothing
sounds.

The tonal layer of an effect -- an atmosphere's held tone above all -- is mapped into the mode, and never
onto the b2 or the leading tone: a minor seventh the mode lacks becomes the fifth, not the major seventh.

The two send effects are a short room and a long hall, both feedback delay networks. Their returns
are ducked with the kick like everything else, and high-passed: a reverb tail in the bass register is
mud.

== Mixer / Master ==

A channel strip per part: its pan (the six voices), its sends to the room, the hall and its delay or
effects bus, its duck under the kick, the mute ("M") and the fader, beside a meter of what the part
puts into the mix -- after fader, trance gate and duck, before the master. The green-to-red bar is the
RMS level, the line above it the peak, held for a second and a half; the figure under the meter is
that peak in dBFS. The knobs of a strip are the same parameters its synth page shows. Kick and bass
have no strip level of their own: their fader is the synth's level.

Beside the strips the track gain and the sidechain's times, and the master chain: bus compressor,
mono bass, a two-times oversampled knee clipper, a lookahead true-peak limiter and a safety clip,
metered to ITU-R BS.1770.

Auto Gain renders four four-bar windows of the track through the whole master chain, measures them
and offsets the master gain so that the output meets the loudness target. It runs when a track is
planned, so it uses the knobs as they stand at that moment.

== Perform ==

Four macros, meant for a hand rather than a mouse.

They are immediate parameter moves rather than score events, and that is measured rather than
convenient: the engine's control ring is first in, first out and the composer keeps it filled eight
bars ahead, so an event pushed into it now would not be heard for about thirteen seconds. A macro
therefore writes the knob itself, as an offset in the knob's normalised domain, and puts it back
exactly where it was when you let go. The composer's own ramps keep riding on top.

Drop-out is a press, not a hold: it takes the kick and the bass out and lets them back in on the next
bar line. Stutter is held. Filter Sweep and Gate Depth stay where you leave them, and a double click
puts them back to neutral.

Keyboard: notes from a MIDI keyboard can play one voice -- the acid or any of the six melodic voices,
or "By channel" (channel 1 the acid, 2 the lead ... 7 the drone) -- with the sound that voice's tab
has. Replace leaves the generator's notes of that voice out, Layer plays over them. The keyboard plays
while the set runs.

MIDI: every knob, switch and chooser, and the four macros, can be put on a hardware controller. Right
click the control, choose MIDI Learn and turn the knob on your controller. The MIDI group lists every
binding and forgets them all at once.

Rate what you hear: Good here and Bad here write a verdict with a note into your ratings. Learn from
my ratings fits your taste to them and, once you confirm, weights the choices the style leaves open
towards what you liked. The genre's rules always stay above your taste.

== Gallery ==

Your saved sets, each with a picture of its form. Save to gallery keeps the set as it stands -- seed,
style, arc, knobs, locks and rerolls -- and a click on an entry loads it back. The files are ordinary
.phosset files in your Sets folder; Open folder shows it.

== Sound presets ==

Every synth tab starts with a Sound group: the synth's presets in a chooser, grouped by kind, a
Save... button for presets of your own, and the Own Sound switch.

Normally the generator designs a new sound for every synth in every track. Own Sound keeps it from
doing that: the synth then sounds exactly as its tab says, in every track. Choosing a preset switches
Own Sound on for you, because otherwise the next track would replace the preset with a sound of its
own. A preset sets the synth's sound and leaves its level, its ducking and -- on the melodic voices --
its high pass and trance gate alone, since those are where the mix and the form put the voice. Undo
takes a preset back.

The factory presets are the generator's own sound recipes, laid out on purpose: points spread evenly
over each synth's five character directions (brightness, softness, thickness, space and motion for a
voice; length, punch, body, grit and click for the kick), every oscillator of a voice's palette with
them, every wavetable of its families twice. A preset is named after its strongest direction and the
synth's role -- "Razor Comet" is a hard lead, "Nebula Aurora" a wide pad, "Tectonic Anvil" a kick with
body, and a kick of the resonant engine has a struck noun ("Gong", "Bell"). The kick, the bass and the
acid are grouped by character, the voices by oscillator and wavetable family: some 90 to 110 presets
for kick, bass and acid, several hundred for each voice. Your own presets are listed under User and
live in the Presets folder of your Phosphene user folder.

== Shortcuts ==

    F1               help (this page); Esc closes it
    F11              full screen (standalone)
    Ctrl+Z           undo
    Ctrl+Y           redo (also Ctrl+Shift+Z)
    right click      MIDI Learn / Forget MIDI on any control
    double click     a knob back to its default

The window can be dragged to any size; the whole panel zooms with it. The standalone's title bar has a
maximise button, and Full screen in the header (or F11) fills the screen.

== Updates ==

Phosphene looks for a newer release at most once a day, when its window opens, by asking GitHub for
the latest release of the project -- nothing else is sent, and nothing is installed by itself. When
there is one, the header says so and a click opens the release page. The check can be switched off
here on the help page, and Check now asks at once.
